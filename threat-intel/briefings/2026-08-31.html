📋 This Week's Threat Summary

Critical developments August 25 – 31: PaperCut disclosed active exploitation of a two-vulnerability chain in its widely deployed NG and MF print management servers — CVE-2026-81578 (CVSS 8.8, an access-control gap reaching admin functions before authentication finishes) and CVE-2026-82078 (CVSS 9.4, unsafe dynamic class-loading that lets an attacker run arbitrary Java bytecode). PaperCut shipped an emergency patch on August 28, but researchers demonstrated a bypass within days, forcing a second emergency patch for versions 24, 25, and 26 the same week. Separately, medical device manufacturer Boston Scientific disclosed on August 25 that a cyberattack was causing a "global disruption" to its operations, cutting off information systems and the order-processing and shipping pipeline; the company filed an SEC Form 8-K, its shares dropped as much as 6%, and as of this writing it has not confirmed whether ransomware was involved or received a ransom demand. Finally, the Rhysida ransomware group claimed on August 28 to have stolen 5.79TB (roughly 1.44 million files) from Berlin's state government network — including judicial documents, emergency response plans, and credentials for nearly 6,000 accounts — and is demanding 30 BTC (~$2.3M) with a one-week deadline; Berlin's government has publicly refused to pay, and officials say election systems for the September 20 vote were not affected.

🔴 PaperCut NG/MF Exploit Chain Survives First Patch — Second Emergency Fix Ships Within Days

🆕 CVE-2026-81578 & CVE-2026-82078: Auth-Bypass-to-RCE Chain in PaperCut Print Management Servers (Active Exploitation Disclosed August 27; Second Emergency Patch August 28, 2026)

PaperCut Software published an urgent security advisory on August 27 confirming active exploitation of a chain affecting all supported versions of PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF. CVE-2026-81578 (CVSS 8.8) is an improper access-control flaw in the web management interface that lets an unauthenticated remote request reach administrative functions before the server finishes validating access; CVE-2026-82078 (CVSS 9.4) is an unsafe dynamic class-loading weakness in the product's database utilities, where driver classes load without being checked against an allowlist, letting an attacker who can alter configuration parameters run arbitrary Java bytecode inside the application server process. PaperCut shipped an emergency patch for versions 25 and 26 on August 28 at 02:10 AEST, with version 24 following the same day — but security researchers demonstrated the first fix could be bypassed, and PaperCut issued a second emergency patch days later once exploitation continued.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: PaperCut NG/MF is a near-ubiquitous print-management deployment in SMB and mid-market environments — exactly the install base this portfolio's MSP clients manage — and it has a documented history as a ransomware entry point (the 2023 PaperCut breaches). A vendor patch that gets bypassed within days is a strong, dated talking point for why "patched" and "current" aren't synonymous, and a good prompt for EDR/MDR account teams to confirm which managed endpoints are running PaperCut and on what version, rather than assuming print servers are out of scope for endpoint monitoring.

🔴 Boston Scientific Discloses "Global Disruption" From Ongoing Cyberattack

🆕 Medical Device Manufacturer Loses Access to Order-Processing and Shipping Systems (Disclosed August 25, 2026)

Boston Scientific identified a cyberattack on August 25 and filed an SEC Form 8-K disclosing a "global disruption" to its operations. The intrusion cut off access to information systems and business applications company-wide, including the systems used to process and ship customer orders; the company activated incident-response procedures and engaged a third-party cybersecurity firm to assess, contain, and scope the activity, with no restoration timeline offered as of this writing. Boston Scientific shares fell as much as 6% in early trading. The company has not disclosed whether ransomware was involved, how initial access occurred, or whether a ransom demand was received, and no threat actor has claimed responsibility.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: This is a live, unresolved incident rather than a closed case study, and the details that matter for MSP/SMB clients — attack vector, ransomware confirmation, ransom demand — aren't public yet. Worth monitoring for follow-up reporting; in the meantime it's a timely, real-world prompt for account teams fielding "could this happen to us" questions, particularly for clients in manufacturing, healthcare, and distribution where order/shipping-system downtime has an immediate revenue impact distinct from a typical data-breach conversation.

🟡 Rhysida Claims 5.79TB Berlin State Government Breach Weeks Before Elections

🆕 Ransom Demand of 30 BTC Refused; Election Infrastructure Reported Unaffected (Claim Posted August 28, 2026)

The Rhysida ransomware group posted a claim on its dark-web leak site on August 28 alleging a major breach of Berlin's state government IT network, asserting exfiltration of 5.79TB of data across roughly 1.44 million files — including judicial documents, emergency response plans, critical-infrastructure details, 80,000 administrative fine proceedings, 46,500 contracts, and nearly 6,000 files containing login credentials, plus personal data tied to over 12,000 individuals. Rhysida is demanding 30 BTC (approximately $2.3 million) with a one-week deadline before threatening to publish the data. Berlin's mayor and interior senator issued a joint statement refusing to pay, stating the government "will not submit to extortion." The attack lands less than a month before Berlin's September 20 city-state elections, though officials say election systems and election-related data were not affected.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: A government target that publicly refuses a multimillion-dollar ransom is a useful, concrete example for client conversations about ransom-payment posture and its consequences — data exposure risk versus payment — and reinforces that credential-heavy administrative data (contracts, fine records, login credentials) is exactly what ransomware crews now monetize even when the primary extortion angle is data leverage rather than encryption.

📌 Strategic Context: "Patched" Isn't a Finish Line, and Everyday MSP Software Is Still the Softest Target

The PaperCut chain is this week's clearest reminder that a vendor's first emergency patch is not automatically the end of the story — researchers bypassed it within days, and PaperCut had to ship a second fix while exploitation was still active, echoing the pattern from recent briefings where patch-to-exploitation windows keep collapsing (three days for SAP Commerce Cloud, five for Cisco ASA/FTD). Unlike the vCenter and Entra ID stories from prior weeks, PaperCut sits squarely inside the everyday SMB/MSP software stack rather than enterprise infrastructure, which makes it a more direct fit for this portfolio's client base than most of what typically leads these briefings. The Boston Scientific and Berlin incidents, meanwhile, are a useful before/after pair on transparency: one is an unresolved, unconfirmed-cause disruption at a major manufacturer, the other a claimed, dated, quantified breach at a government body that has chosen to disclose its refusal to pay rather than stay silent. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: Worth confirming with the MDR/EDR and RMM-adjacent teams whether current external-attack-surface and vulnerability-scanning coverage flags PaperCut instances specifically, given its recurring role as a ransomware entry point since 2023.
