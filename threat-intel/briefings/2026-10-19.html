📋 This Week's Threat Summary

Critical development: Microsoft's October 2026 Patch Tuesday fixed a record 1,413 CVEs (counts vary by tracker, 1,407–1,413), including one actively exploited zero-day, CVE-2026-96951, a Windows shell Mark-of-the-Web security feature bypass (CVSS 8.8). CISA added it to KEV with an October 16 federal deadline.

🔴 Record Patch Tuesday and Exploited Mark-of-the-Web Bypass

🆕 CVE-2026-96951: Windows shell MotW bypass exploited (Patch Tuesday October 13–14, 2026)
Per SecurityWeek, Microsoft patched 1,413 CVEs (1,102 Windows, 134 Office, 61 developer tools, 36 SharePoint Server, plus Exchange, Azure and Defender). The exploited zero-day lets an attacker evade Mark-of-the-Web protections that trigger SmartScreen and Office Protected View warnings on downloaded files. Two further flaws were publicly disclosed before fixes: CVE-2026-108398 (WDAC bypass; exploitation judged unlikely) and CVE-2026-92707 (MessagePack-CSharp denial of service). The article attributes the record volume largely to AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and says the 2026 total has passed 4,000, more than all of 2025.
PORTFOLIO IMPACT: MotW bypasses are a staple of phishing and malware-delivery chains, so they weaken the file-download warnings SMB users rely on. This strengthens the case for EP/EDR behavioral detection on downloaded-file execution, DNS filtering of payload hosts, and SAT content on attachments. The patch volume itself is a burden for MSPs; prioritize CVE-2026-96951 and internet-facing components first.
