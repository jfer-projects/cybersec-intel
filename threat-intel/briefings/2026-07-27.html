🔴 Check Point SmartConsole Authentication-Bypass Zero-Day Under CISA Emergency Directive

🆕 CVE-2026-16232 (CVSS 9.1) Lets Unauthenticated Attackers Obtain Admin-Privileged Login Tokens; Actively Exploited (Disclosed July 22–23, 2026)

Check Point disclosed CVE-2026-16232, a critical authentication-bypass vulnerability in SmartConsole affecting Security Management Servers and Multi-Domain Security Management Servers, that allows an unauthenticated attacker to obtain an application login token usable to authenticate with full administrator privileges. Exploitation requires the Management Server's IP to be exposed to the internet with no restrictions on Trusted Clients. Check Point confirmed active exploitation against what it describes as a small number of customers; once inside, attackers can change security configuration and policy, add or remove access rules, alter VPN gateway configurations, push policy updates to every managed gateway simultaneously, and export the full managed-infrastructure configuration, including encryption keys and certificate authorities used for VPN authentication. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and issued Binding Operational Directive 26-04, ordering Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to apply the July 22 Jumbo hotfix by July 25. Check Point additionally recommends restricting Trusted Clients to known IP addresses/subnets and firewalling management access.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: A single authentication-bypass flaw that hands out admin-level tokens for a centralized security-management console is a high-value target precisely because it controls policy for every downstream gateway at once — the same "one compromise, many downstream effects" pattern that makes MSP-managed infrastructure attractive to attackers. This is a useful, concrete talking point for EDR/MDR conversations about why centralized management-plane exposure (not just endpoints) needs its own hardening and monitoring posture.

🔴 ServiceNow AI Platform Pre-Auth RCE Exploited in the Wild

🆕 CVE-2026-6875: Unauthenticated Script-Sandbox Escape Leads to Remote Code Execution; Exploitation Began July 17, 2026

Threat intelligence firm Defused reported that attackers have begun exploiting CVE-2026-6875, a critical pre-authentication code-injection vulnerability in the ServiceNow AI Platform that lets an unauthenticated attacker escape ServiceNow's script sandbox and execute code remotely on a targeted instance. Searchlight Cyber researchers reported the flaw to ServiceNow in early April 2026; ServiceNow shipped a fix to hosted instances the following day and rolled out patches to self-hosted customers and partners through June. The vulnerability's existence became public on July 13, and in-the-wild exploitation began four days later, on July 17. The delay between silent patching and public disclosure — followed quickly by active exploitation once details circulated — mirrors the same "patch-then-disclose" exploitation window seen with other high-value SaaS platforms this year.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: ServiceNow is widely used for IT service management and increasingly for AI-agent workflows, making it both a high-value target and, per the Abbott Laboratories incident below, a system threat actors specifically ask about when negotiating extortion. Instances still running unpatched self-hosted deployments (patches available since June) remain exposed nearly six weeks after fixes shipped — a useful, concrete example for MDR conversations about the gap between "patch available" and "patch applied" in complex enterprise SaaS/PaaS estates.

🟠 Hugging Face Breach Attributed to an Autonomous AI Agent

🆕 Unauthorized Access to Internal Datasets and Service Credentials, Carried Out by an Autonomous Agent System (Disclosed July 16, 2026)

Hugging Face, the widely used platform for sharing open-source machine-learning models and datasets, disclosed that it identified unauthorized access to some internal datasets and to several credentials used by its services, and attributed the intrusion to an autonomous AI agent system rather than a conventional manual attack chain. Details on the specific agent tooling and full scope of accessed data remain limited as investigation continues.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: This is a notable data point in a broader pattern this month — following the July 21 ENCFORGE/JADEPUFFER ransomware campaign against AI-infrastructure targets (Langflow) covered in last week's briefing — of AI-native attack tooling and AI-infrastructure targets moving from theoretical to actively observed. As MSP clients adopt AI-agent tooling internally, credential and dataset exposure tied to that tooling is becoming a distinct, dated risk category rather than a hypothetical one.

🟠 Abbott Laboratories Faces Dual Extortion Claims and a Federal Class Action

🆕 Vishing-Enabled Entra SSO Compromise (ShinyHunters) and a Separate LabCentral Portal Claim (ShadowByt3$); Class Action Filed This Week (Incident Ongoing Since Mid-June, Litigation July 2026)

Abbott Laboratories is investigating two separate incidents: ShinyHunters claims it used a vishing attack against several Abbott employees in mid-June to compromise a corporate Microsoft Entra single sign-on account, then pivoted to data connected to Microsoft Entra, ServiceNow, SharePoint, Databricks, and Coupa — claiming more than 30 million rows of customer PII (names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, dates of birth, over one million Social Security numbers) plus over 22 million client notes containing doctor-patient conversations and more than 20 million medical orders. Separately, a threat actor calling itself ShadowByt3$ claims a distinct breach of Abbott's LabCentral portal. ShinyHunters initially threatened to publish stolen files July 18, later extended to July 21 amid negotiations; as of this week neither group has leaked data, but a proposed federal class action has now been filed against Abbott and its Exact Sciences cancer-diagnostics business over the data-theft claims. Abbott states the incidents have not affected product availability, manufacturing, or patient services.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: Voice phishing targeting help-desk/SSO-reset workflows to compromise a single Entra identity — and from there pivot across five-plus connected SaaS platforms (Entra, ServiceNow, SharePoint, Databricks, Coupa) — is a textbook illustration of identity-centric lateral movement that endpoint telemetry alone won't catch. This is a strong, name-recognizable, ongoing example for SAT/HRM conversations about vishing/help-desk social-engineering scenarios, and for MDR conversations about identity-anomaly detection across connected SaaS estates rather than single-platform monitoring.

🟡 Watchlist: Ransomware Ecosystem Now 146 Active Groups

🟡 Ransomware Market Fragmentation Continues — 146 Active Groups as of June, Qilin Now Largest Operator; EMEA Healthcare Supply Chains Under Elevated Targeting
🏷 Ransomware-as-a-Service Ecosystem  |  📅 Published July 24, 2026 (covering April 2025 – March 2026 data)
New ecosystem reporting shows 61 new ransomware groups entered the market between April 2025 and March 2026 — averaging more than one new group per week — bringing the count of active threat groups to 146 by June 2026. The reporting period recorded 7,551 ransomware victims, with the five largest groups accounting for 43.6% of all victims; disclosures rose 60% in the second half of the period compared to the first, and Qilin overtook prior leaders to become the largest single operator by volume. A companion report flagged elevated ransomware targeting of EMEA healthcare supply chains specifically. PORTFOLIO IMPACT: Continues the fragmentation trend flagged in the July 20 briefing's "Gentlemen" coverage — with 146 groups now active and new entrants arriving weekly, threat-intelligence feeds and MDR playbooks built around a short list of "known major" groups will increasingly miss newer, faster-scaling operators; broader behavioral/TTP-based detection remains more durable than group-specific IOC lists alone.

📌 Strategic Context: AI Infrastructure and Agentic Tooling Are Becoming a Standing Attack Surface

Three of this week's items touch AI tooling or AI-adjacent infrastructure directly: the ServiceNow flaw sits in the vendor's "AI Platform" product line, the Hugging Face breach is attributed to an autonomous AI agent rather than a human-driven attack chain, and this continues directly from last week's ENCFORGE/JADEPUFFER ransomware campaign against Langflow AI-infrastructure servers. Combined with Check Point's management-plane compromise and Abbott's identity-pivot-across-five-SaaS-platforms extortion case, the pattern for this month is that both the targets (AI platforms, centralized management consoles, connected SaaS estates) and in at least one case the attacker tooling itself (an autonomous agent) are shifting away from the traditional single-endpoint model. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: For MDR and threat-intelligence conversations, this reinforces that detection strategy needs visibility into AI-agent and AI-platform activity specifically — not just endpoint and network telemetry — as clients increasingly deploy AI tooling internally and become targets whose exposure now include the AI systems themselves.
