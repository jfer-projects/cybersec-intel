🔴 Lazarus Group Exploited Windows Kernel Zero-Day for Five Weeks Before August Patch Tuesday Fix

🆕 CVE-2026-68820: AFD.sys Use-After-Free Chained With FudModule v3.1 Rootkit and ForestTiger Backdoor via Fake Job-Offer Lures (Patched August 11, 2026)

Microsoft's August Patch Tuesday fixed roughly 400+ CVEs, including one confirmed exploited-in-the-wild zero-day: CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free vulnerability in the Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (afd.sys), the kernel-mode driver underlying the Windows Sockets API. Microsoft confirmed the bug on July 31 and shipped a fix August 11 — but a compiled FudModule rootkit artifact recovered by researchers carries a July 7 timestamp, indicating North Korea's Lazarus Group had a working exploit at least five weeks before the patch. The observed campaign targeted defense, aerospace, and aviation sector employees across Europe, India, and Brazil using fake LinkedIn job offers in an Operation Dream Job-style social-engineering lure. Successful exploitation grants SYSTEM-level access, which Lazarus used to load FudModule v3.1 — an updated variant of its signature rootkit that strips telemetry callbacks, disables minifilters, kills the NT Kernel Logger, and blinds more than 90 ETW (Event Tracing for Windows) providers, effectively going dark to most endpoint monitoring. The chain finishes with ForestTiger, a well-documented Lazarus backdoor that provides long-term remote access to the compromised host.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: FudModule's specific design goal — blinding ETW providers and killing kernel logging — means detection has to happen before or during the privilege-escalation step, not by relying on telemetry the rootkit is built to suppress; this is a concrete, dated example for EDR conversations about why kernel-level exploit and driver-tampering detection matters independent of post-compromise log analysis. The five-week gap between first observed exploitation and patch availability is also a reminder that MSPs serving defense-adjacent, aerospace, or manufacturing clients should treat unusual LinkedIn/recruiting-themed outreach to technical staff as a named, current threat vector worth specific security-awareness-training content — not a generic phishing example.

🔴 Cisco ASA/FTD Remote Access VPN Flaw Exploited to Crash Devices — Second Cisco KEV Entry in Two Weeks

🆕 CVE-2026-20349: Unauthenticated Denial-of-Service in Remote Access SSL VPN Service (Added to CISA KEV August 11, 2026)

Cisco disclosed a high-severity (CVSS 8.6) denial-of-service vulnerability in the Remote Access SSL VPN service of Secure Firewall ASA and Secure Firewall Threat Defense (FTD) software, caused by insufficient error checking (improper heap-memory clearing, CWE-244) when processing HTTP requests. An unauthenticated remote attacker can send a single crafted HTTP request to a device with SSL listen sockets enabled — including IKEv2 Remote Access VPN with client services, SSL VPN, or Zero Trust Network Access configurations — to force an unscheduled reload. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 11, confirming active exploitation, the same day as the Windows AFD.sys zero-day above and a Metabase SQL injection flaw (CVE-2026-72898). Cisco has shipped fixed FTD and ASA releases; no workaround exists short of patching.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: This is the second Cisco security-appliance vulnerability to land in CISA's KEV catalog in as many weeks — last week's Secure FMC static-credential authentication bypass, this week a different product family (ASA/FTD) with a different flaw class (DoS rather than auth bypass). Two distinct Cisco edge-device CVEs in consecutive weeks is a pattern worth flagging directly to MSPs running Cisco firewalls alongside our EDR/MDR/DNS products: edge VPN and firewall appliances remain the most consistently exploited entry point of 2026, and patch-verification sweeps for Cisco-specific infrastructure are now warranted as a standing monthly check rather than a one-time response to a single CVE.

🟡 CISA Joint Advisory: Gunra Ransomware Has Breached 51+ Organizations via Unpatched Fortinet Firewalls

🆕 #StopRansomware Advisory AA26-222A: Conti-Derived RaaS Exploits Two Already-Patched Fortinet Auth-Bypass CVEs; Linux Encryptor Has a Recoverable Flaw (August 10, 2026)

CISA, the FBI, the Department of Defense Cyber Crime Center, NSA, the U.S. Secret Service, and South Korea's National Police Agency issued a joint #StopRansomware advisory on Gunra, a double-extortion ransomware-as-a-service operation built on leaked Conti source code that first appeared in April 2025. The advisory's most actionable finding is the entry point: Gunra affiliates gained initial access primarily by exploiting two authentication-bypass vulnerabilities in Fortinet FortiOS and FortiProxy — CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472 — both of which already have vendor patches, meaning every documented intrusion path in the advisory was preventable. From there, actors moved through stolen accounts and sessions, exfiltrated business data, and encrypted systems on both Windows (appending .ENCRT or .CRYPT) and Linux (.GNRA) environments. Gunra has struck at least 51 organizations — hospitals, government agencies, and financial institutions — across the Americas, Europe, the Middle East, Africa, and Asia-Pacific, with documented ransom demands typically exceeding $10 million. Separately, researchers previously confirmed Gunra's Linux encryptor contains a cryptographic implementation flaw that allows victims to recover encrypted files without paying.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: Gunra is the third ransomware operation in as many weeks tracked in this briefing whose initial access ran through an unpatched edge appliance rather than social engineering or a novel exploit — a direct, repeatable talking point for MDR/MXDR conversations about why external attack-surface and patch-verification monitoring belongs alongside endpoint detection, not as a separate afterthought. For any MSP client running Fortinet firewalls, this advisory is a specific, dated, named-CVE prompt to verify FortiOS/FortiProxy patch status immediately rather than waiting for a broader audit cycle; the free Linux-decryption angle is also a useful, concrete data point for incident-response conversations with affected clients before considering payment.

📌 Strategic Context: Edge Devices and Kernel-Level Stealth Were This Week's Common Thread

Two distinct patterns run through this week's items. First, edge and management infrastructure keeps being the entry point rather than the endpoint: a Cisco ASA/FTD VPN flaw and Gunra's Fortinet exploitation mark the third and fourth consecutive week this briefing has covered an edge-appliance compromise (following N-able N-central and Cisco FMC), reinforcing that perimeter and management-plane patch verification deserves the same monitoring cadence as endpoint fleets. Second, the Lazarus campaign shows nation-state actors investing specifically in defeating telemetry itself — FudModule v3.1's ETW-blinding is a direct answer to the behavioral-detection approaches most EDR vendors (including ours) lean on. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: Together these argue for two concrete checks worth raising internally: whether our EDR's detection logic has any dependency on ETW providers that a sufficiently privileged rootkit could disable, and whether our MDR/DNS offerings include automated verification that edge VPN/firewall appliances in a client's environment are on current patch levels rather than relying on the client to self-report.
