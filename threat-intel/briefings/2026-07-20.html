🔴 Microsoft's Largest-Ever Patch Tuesday Ships Two Actively Exploited Zero-Days

🆕 CVE-2026-56155 (ADFS) and CVE-2026-56164 (SharePoint) Exploited in the Wild; Third Straight Week of Active SharePoint Exploitation (July 14, 2026)

Microsoft's July 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed a record 622 CVEs across Windows, Office, Azure, SharePoint, and Exchange — the largest single Patch Tuesday in company history. Two of the disclosed flaws were already being exploited as zero-days: CVE-2026-56155, an elevation-of-privilege flaw in Active Directory Federation Services (CVSS 7.8) that lets an attacker gain administrator privileges, credited to Microsoft's own Detection and Response Team; and CVE-2026-56164, an elevation-of-privilege flaw in SharePoint Server (CVSS 5.3, but network-exploitable with no prior privileges required) affecting SharePoint 2016, 2019, and Subscription Edition. Attackers have reportedly been chaining CVE-2026-56164 with older, previously disclosed SharePoint weaknesses to steal IIS machine keys, establish persistence, and deploy follow-on malware. CISA added both CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on July 14, the same day patches shipped, with a remediation deadline of July 17 for federal agencies. Separately, CISA added a further SharePoint deserialization-of-untrusted-data flaw, CVE-2026-58644, to KEV on July 16.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: This is the third consecutive week SharePoint has been the subject of active-exploitation reporting in this briefing (following the June 29 SharePoint RCE coverage and July 13's HSIN breach, which touched an internal SharePoint system) — reinforcing that on-premises SharePoint deployments remain a standing, recurring target rather than a one-off event. The scale of this Patch Tuesday alone (622 CVEs) is also a strong, dated talking point for vulnerability-aware EDR and MDR conversations: patch volume at this scale makes manual triage increasingly unrealistic for lean MSP-serviced IT teams, strengthening the case for automated detection that assumes some population of endpoints will run unpatched for a period after disclosure.

🔴 SonicWall SMA VPN Zero-Days Exploited by Suspected Chinese Actor Since Late June

🆕 CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0) and CVE-2026-15410 Chained for Root Access; Custom Malware Deployed by UTA0533 (Disclosed July 17, 2026)

Threat intelligence firm Volexity disclosed on July 17 that a previously undocumented, suspected Chinese threat actor it tracks as UTA0533 has been exploiting two zero-day vulnerabilities in SonicWall Secure Mobile Access (SMA) 1000-series VPN appliances since at least June 22, 2026. CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0) and CVE-2026-15410 (CVSS 7.2) can be chained to achieve arbitrary command execution and full device takeover. With root access, UTA0533 deployed custom malware — including a tool dubbed KNUCKLEBALL, which loaded two Java-based utilities into a legitimate SonicWall process: the open-source proxy tool Suo5 and a Behinder-like webshell called ORANGETAIL — enabling the group to access stored and cached credentials, capture network traffic, and intercept credentials processed by the appliance. SonicWall patched both vulnerabilities this week and confirmed active exploitation. Both CVEs were added to CISA's KEV catalog on July 14.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: VPN and remote-access appliances remain a consistently high-value target for well-resourced threat actors specifically because they sit at the network edge and hold credential material for everything behind them — a pattern directly relevant to MSPs managing remote-access infrastructure for SMB clients. The month-long gap between first exploitation (June 22) and public disclosure/patch (July 17) is a reminder that "no CVE yet" does not mean "not being actively exploited" — behavioral detection on VPN/edge appliances and credential-use anomaly monitoring remain necessary even absent a published vulnerability.

🟠 EY Discloses Breach of Third-Party IT Support Platform, Client Tax Data Exposed

🆕 Unauthorized Access to IT Support Ticketing Platform Exposes Client SSNs, Driver's License Numbers, and Tax/Investment Records (Disclosed July 15, 2026)

Ernst & Young (EY) notified clients and filed breach notifications with the California Attorney General's office on July 15 after an unauthorized third party accessed a support ticketing platform used by EY's IT staff between March 28 and April 12, 2026, downloading documents belonging to a number of EY clients. EY identified the anomalous activity on April 23 and triggered incident response at that time; the roughly three-month gap before public disclosure is consistent with investigation and legal-notification timelines rather than a newly discovered intrusion. Exposed data includes names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, driver's license numbers, email addresses, phone numbers, and financial information tied to individuals' investment holdings with EY's institutional clients, along with data used in preparing tax filings. EY is offering affected individuals 24 months of complimentary Experian IdentityWorks credit and identity monitoring and states it has no current evidence of data misuse.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: A breach at a Big Four professional services firm via a third-party IT support tool is a useful, concrete illustration of third-party/vendor risk — the initial foothold was not EY's own core systems but a support platform used by its IT staff, a pattern common among MSP environments where ticketing, RMM, and helpdesk tools represent a similarly attractive lateral-entry point. For clients in accounting, tax preparation, financial services, or any regulated data-handling vertical, this is a timely, name-recognizable example to reference when discussing third-party/vendor risk assessment and supply-chain attack surface.

🟡 Fortinet FortiSandbox Command Injection Flaws Under Active Exploitation

🆕 CVE-2026-39808 (CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2026-25089 Added to CISA KEV; Federal Patch Order Issued (July 16–17, 2026)

CISA added two Fortinet FortiSandbox OS command-injection vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on July 16: CVE-2026-39808 (CVSS 9.8), an unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw affecting FortiSandbox versions 4.4.0–4.4.8 and FortiSandbox PaaS, where attackers inject shell metacharacters into an unsanitized parameter to execute commands with system-level privileges; and CVE-2026-25089, a related command-injection flaw in the FortiSandbox web UI affecting FortiSandbox, FortiSandbox Cloud, and FortiSandbox PaaS. Exploitation of CVE-2026-39808 was first observed by threat trackers on June 12, and network monitoring firm CrowdSec has logged dozens of unique IP addresses actively probing for the vulnerabilities. CISA issued a patch order to federal agencies on July 17. Fortinet has shipped fixes in FortiSandbox 4.4.9+ and 5.0.6+.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: FortiSandbox appliances are typically deployed for malware detonation/analysis and often sit with elevated network trust — an unauthenticated RCE against this class of device is a meaningful lateral-movement risk for any client running Fortinet security infrastructure. Combined with the SonicWall and SharePoint items above, this is the third distinct piece of widely deployed security/network infrastructure (VPN gateway, collaboration platform, sandboxing appliance) shown to be under active exploitation this week alone — reinforcing that "security appliance" status does not exempt a product from being a primary attack vector.

🟡 Watchlist: Ransomware Ecosystem Notes

🟡 "The Gentlemen" Ransomware Group Now Second-Most-Active Operation Globally, Scaling Faster Than Any Tracked Group on Record
🏷 Ransomware-as-a-Service Ecosystem  |  📅 Ongoing, Q1–Q2 2026 trend data
The Gentlemen, a ransomware and extortion operation that emerged publicly in the second half of 2025, has scaled from roughly 40 victims in Q4 2025 to well over 150 in Q1 2026 alone — a growth curve researchers describe as reaching in about five months a scale that took Akira twelve months and Qilin eighteen months to achieve, comparable to the early trajectory of LockBit 3.0. Leak-site monitoring places The Gentlemen among the top two most active ransomware groups globally in 2026, targeting Windows, Linux, ESXi, and NAS environments. PORTFOLIO IMPACT: The Gentlemen's growth rate is a useful, concrete data point for MDR and endpoint conversations emphasizing that new ransomware-as-a-service operations can now reach top-tier attack volume within months rather than years — patch-and-detect cadences built around "established" threat actor lists risk missing newer, faster-scaling groups.

📌 Strategic Context: Edge and Collaboration Infrastructure Is the Common Thread

Every major technical item this week involves widely deployed, internet-facing infrastructure that sits at a network edge or collaboration layer rather than a traditional endpoint: SharePoint (collaboration/RCE, third consecutive week), SonicWall SMA (VPN gateway, root-level compromise), and FortiSandbox (security appliance, unauthenticated RCE). This continues directly from the patch-lag and edge-infrastructure themes flagged in the July 6 and July 13 briefings — the pattern is durable enough now to treat as a standing assumption rather than a one-off trend. The EY breach adds a complementary angle: the entry point there was a third-party IT support tool, not core infrastructure, underscoring that vendor/supply-chain risk assessment needs to extend to helpdesk and ticketing tools MSPs themselves rely on daily. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: For MDR and EDR conversations, the consistent message across three straight weeks is that perimeter and collaboration infrastructure — VPNs, SharePoint, security appliances, and third-party support tools — now represents as much or more exploitation risk as the endpoint itself, and detection strategy should weight these systems accordingly rather than treating endpoint telemetry as sufficient on its own.
