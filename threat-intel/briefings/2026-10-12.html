🔴 Apache Struts Command Injection Exploited in the Wild

🆕 CVE-2016-3081: Added to CISA KEV October 8, 2026 (CWE-77)
CISA added the Apache Struts flaw to KEV on October 8 with a federal remediation deadline of October 11 under BOD 26-04, noting attackers are using it in real-world attacks and that the entry requires forensic triage to determine whether systems were already accessed. When Dynamic Method Invocation is enabled, a crafted request abusing the method prefix can execute OS commands. Affected: 2.3.19–2.3.20.2, 2.3.21–2.3.24.1 and 2.3.25–2.3.28; fixed in 2.3.20.3, 2.3.24.3 and 2.3.28.1. Mitigations if patching is delayed: disable DMI, restrict public access, add network controls. Hunt for requests containing "method:" values, unexpected child processes from Java services, new scheduled tasks and suspicious outbound traffic; preserve logs first. No threat actor attribution or campaign details were published.
PORTFOLIO IMPACT: Struts sits inside many line-of-business and vendor-bundled Java apps at SMB customers, often unpatched and invisible to inventory. Post-exploitation (web shells, credential theft, lateral movement) is where EDR/MDR detections apply; MSPs should confirm Java-process child-process alerting is enabled and scan customers for Struts.

🟡 Legacy Flaws Join KEV — BIND, ProFTPD, ONLYOFFICE, Strapi, Zammad

🆕 Older CVEs Confirmed Exploited (week of October 6, 2026)
A KEV tracker lists these additions alongside Struts: ProFTPD CVE-2015-3306 (improper access control), ISC BIND CVE-2015-5477 (data processing error), ONLYOFFICE Docs Server CVE-2021-3199 (path traversal), Strapi CVE-2023-22894 (cleartext storage of sensitive information) and Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490 (session fixation, improper privilege management). The tracker is internally inconsistent (headline says 6 CVEs, body lists 8) and gives no add dates; verify against the CISA catalog before acting. The Struts and NetScaler entries are covered separately.
PORTFOLIO IMPACT: BIND is core DNS software; MSP customers self-hosting resolvers should confirm patched versions, and it reinforces the case for managed DNS protection over self-run resolvers. The set shows attackers sweeping for long-unpatched internet-facing software, which favors vulnerability-aware EDR/MDR coverage.

📌 Strategic Context: Old Vulnerabilities, Fresh Exploitation

After three weeks of perimeter-appliance zero-days, this week shows the other half of the problem: decade-old flaws (Struts 2016, BIND and ProFTPD 2015) being actively exploited. MSP asset inventory, third-party software patching and post-exploitation detection matter as much as zero-day response. Search coverage this week was limited; additional disclosures may be missing.
