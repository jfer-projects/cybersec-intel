🔴 Record September Patch Tuesday Includes Two Windows Zero-Days Exploited Before a Fix Existed

🆕 CVE-2026-85880 (Windows ALPC) & CVE-2026-81963 (Windows Update Stack): Two Actively Exploited Local Privilege-Escalation Zero-Days Among 974 CVEs Patched (September 8, 2026)

Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday fixed a record 974 CVEs — its largest single release to date — including two flaws confirmed exploited in the wild before patches were available. CVE-2026-85880 is a heap buffer overflow in Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call (ALPC) that lets a local, low-privileged attacker escalate to SYSTEM. CVE-2026-81963 is an improper link-resolution ("link following") flaw in the Windows Update Stack, notable as the first Windows Update Stack elevation-of-privilege bug ever exploited as a zero-day. Both carry a CVSS score of 7.8, require low attack complexity and no user interaction, and are being combined with separate code-execution bugs to spread malware and ransomware payloads once initial access is achieved. CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 8, setting a September 22 remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: Local privilege-escalation zero-days are exactly the class of vulnerability that endpoint and EDR telemetry is built to catch post-exploitation, and a record-setting 974-CVE Patch Tuesday is a concrete, dated prompt for MSP account teams to confirm client patch compliance windows and flag the two actively-exploited items specifically, since attackers chain them with other bugs to reach ransomware deployment rather than stopping at privilege escalation alone.

🔴 "StyleSmuggler" — Maximum-Severity Adobe Commerce & Magento Zero-Day Exploited for Three Days Before a Hotfix Existed

🆕 CVE-2026-75650 (CVSS 10.0): Unauthenticated RCE via Template/Style Injection, Actively Exploited Since September 4; Hotfix Shipped September 7, 2026

Adobe disclosed StyleSmuggler, a maximum-severity unauthenticated remote-code-execution zero-day affecting Adobe Commerce (2.4.4–2.4.9), Adobe Commerce B2B (1.3.3–1.5.3), and Magento Open Source (2.4.6–2.4.9). Attackers abuse how Magento processes style properties to smuggle malicious PHP code past existing safeguards, with the payload executed when Magento renders a routine "Payment Transaction Failed Reminder" email — no authentication required. Active exploitation began September 4, three days before Adobe released Hotfix VULN-39341 on September 7; researchers have since observed roughly 500 unique IP addresses sending matching exploit requests, with compromised storefronts receiving Rust-based Linux backdoors and PHP web shells.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: E-commerce is a common SMB/MSP-managed workload, and a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE with a three-day head start over the vendor's own fix — plus confirmed backdoor deployment — is a direct prompt for account teams to identify any managed clients running Adobe Commerce or Magento Open Source, confirm Hotfix VULN-39341 is applied, and check for the Rust-based backdoor and web-shell indicators rather than assuming a clean patch closes an already-open door.

🟡 Sixth Actively Exploited Chrome Zero-Day of 2026 Patched in V8 Engine

🆕 CVE-2026-85046 (CVSS 8.8): V8 Type-Confusion Bug Enables Sandboxed Code Execution via Crafted Web Page (Patched September 3; Added to CISA KEV September 4, 2026)

Google shipped an emergency Chrome update fixing 12 vulnerabilities, including CVE-2026-85046, a high-severity type-confusion flaw in the V8 JavaScript/WebAssembly engine that was already being exploited in the wild. A crafted HTML page could trigger the bug to execute arbitrary code inside Chrome's sandbox. The fix landed in Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83 for Windows and macOS and 152.0.7977.82 for Linux. CISA added the CVE to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 4, with a September 18 remediation deadline for federal agencies. This is the sixth actively exploited Chrome zero-day Google has patched since January 2026.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: A sixth browser zero-day in nine months underscores that browser patch cadence deserves the same urgency as OS patch cadence in managed environments — DNS-layer protection can help block known malicious delivery domains for browser exploit chains, but confirming auto-update is actually functioning on managed endpoints remains the more direct control here.

🟡 Revolut Discloses Breach After Staff Handed Customer Data to Fraudulent Requests From a Hijacked Government Email Domain

🆕 A "Limited" Number of Customers Affected — Passports, IDs, and Financial Details Exposed; No System Intrusion Involved (Disclosed September 12, 2026)

UK-based fintech Revolut confirmed it disclosed sensitive customer data to an unauthorized third party after receiving fraudulent information requests sent from an email address operating inside a genuine government agency's domain. Believing the requests were legitimate legal or regulatory demands, Revolut staff handed over customer identity and contact details — including dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, and copies of passports and driver's licenses, with some cases potentially including verification selfies, account statements, and transaction history — before separately contacting the agency and discovering the requests were fraudulent. Revolut has not disclosed an exact victim count but describes it as a "limited" number of customers, has contacted those affected directly, blocked the fraudulent email address, and reports that its systems and customer funds were unaffected throughout.

PORTFOLIO IMPACT: No firewall, endpoint agent, or DNS filter would have stopped this — a compromised or spoofed government-agency mailbox sent what looked like a routine, authoritative data request, and staff complied without an independent verification step. It's a clean, dated example for client conversations and internal training content: the control that would have mattered here is a verification callback policy for any external data request, which is a security-awareness and process discipline question, not a technology gap.

📌 Strategic Context: Patch Volume Keeps Climbing While the Costliest Breach This Week Needed No Exploit At All

This week's three vulnerability disclosures — a record 974-CVE Patch Tuesday, a maximum-severity Adobe Commerce zero-day, and Chrome's sixth exploited zero-day of the year — continue a pattern of rising patch volume and shrinking time-to-exploit that has defined most of 2026's briefings. All three involve real technical flaws that endpoint, browser, and patch-management tooling are built to address once patches ship, and all three had working exploits circulating before or within days of vendor fixes. The Revolut breach is the useful counterpoint: no vulnerability was involved anywhere in that incident, only a convincing, authoritative-looking email that staff acted on without independent verification. Taken together, the week reinforces a point worth repeating in client conversations — technical patching discipline and human verification discipline are two separate controls, and a portfolio strong on one doesn't imply coverage of the other. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: Worth a short prompt to the SAT/HRM product team on whether current training content and simulated-phishing scenarios cover "fraudulent request from a spoofed or hijacked authoritative domain" as a distinct scenario from generic phishing — the Revolut incident is a concrete, well-covered example to build a module or comms piece around.
