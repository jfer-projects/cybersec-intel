🛡️ Endpoint Protection / 🔍 EDR (2 new items)

🆕 SentinelOne Singularity Agentic Detection and Response (ADR)
🏷 SentinelOne  |  📅 October 21–22, 2026
SentinelOne announced Singularity ADR, which discovers AI agents and MCP servers across endpoints, SaaS and cloud, enforces allow/block and approval policy, and blocks prompt injection, credential exfiltration and tool poisoning inline. Agent activity is joined with EDR telemetry in Storyline, with intent-drift detection and single-agent containment. Coverage reportedly spans Salesforce Agentforce, Copilot Studio, ChatGPT workspace agents and Claude managed agents. A companion Singularity AI SOC (ASOC) announcement was published the same day. No GA date, packaging or pricing disclosed; MSP angle is not addressed by the vendor. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Confirms that agent governance is moving from standalone add-on to an EDR-native capability (see Bitdefender, WithSecure, ESET, CrowdStrike last week). The differentiator is correlation with endpoint telemetry. Decide whether our EP/EDR roadmap needs agent/MCP inventory and where MSPs would price it.
Sources: Help Net Security · SentinelOne press release (via Stockhouse)
🆕 Arctic Wolf Aurora Adaptive Threat Protection (exclusive beta)
🏷 Arctic Wolf  |  📅 October 21, 2026 (Security Operations Summit)
Arctic Wolf introduced an endpoint security layer using intent-based detection over a causal graph of process, file, registry and API activity, with automated interruption and rollback once malicious intent is confirmed. It is designed to run alongside existing third-party EPP/EDR rather than replace it, uses a custom small language model with human oversight, and is in an exclusive beta for current Arctic Wolf customers. Not stated whether MSP partners can access it. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Arctic Wolf is now offering its own endpoint prevention layer on top of an MDR relationship, after Aurora MDR Connect (September 21) aimed at smaller MSPs. The "coexist, don't rip-and-replace" positioning pressures standalone EP vendors in MSP stacks.
Sources: CXOToday

🌐 DNS Protection (2 new items)

🆕 Palo Alto Networks Advanced DNS Security inside Amazon Route 53 Resolver DNS Firewall
🏷 Palo Alto Networks, AWS  |  📅 GA September 29; Palo Alto announcement October 9, 2026
AWS made the integration generally available across 32 regions. It inspects internet-bound queries from AWS workloads with 30+ threat detections (hidden communication, newly registered/fake domains, hijacked domains, infrastructure abuse), purchased via AWS Marketplace and rolled out through License Manager and Firewall Manager. Detections can start in alert mode. Source is a single secondary blog post. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: DNS security is being embedded in the cloud-provider control plane, which is outside the SMB/MSP segment but sets expectations on threat-category depth and newly-registered-domain handling.
Sources: shashi.co (secondary)
🆕 Quad9 to stop serving France on November 24 after beIN piracy-blocking order
🏷 Quad9 (also Mullvad)  |  📅 October 2026
A Paris court ordered Quad9 to block sports-piracy domains under a €1,000/day penalty; Quad9 has appealed and says it will withdraw both filtering and resolution in France on November 24 rather than extend the blocklist globally. About 20 other French suits are reportedly pending. Mullvad closes its public DoH service on November 2 and points users to Quad9, so those users face a second migration. The blocking requirement itself expires November 14. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Regulatory/court-ordered DNS blocking is a live risk for free public resolvers and creates a displacement opportunity with French and EU SMBs and MSPs that rely on Quad9 for filtering. A commercial, jurisdiction-aware DNS filter with admin-controlled policy is the natural alternative to message.
Sources: CyberInsider

📌 Strategic Context

Two weeks of AI-agent security launches have now culminated in SentinelOne tying agent activity into its EDR graph, while Arctic Wolf pushes a coexisting endpoint layer from the MDR side. For an MSP/SMB-focused portfolio the open questions are packaging (module vs. included) and whether agent/MCP inventory is table stakes by 2027. In DNS, the Quad9 withdrawal is a concrete, dated opening in France.
