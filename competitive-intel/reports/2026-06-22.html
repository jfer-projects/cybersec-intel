PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Huntress (MDR competitor for SMBs) gains distribution infrastructure comparable to incumbent vendors. 🔴 Klue/Icarus SaaS Supply Chain Breach Exposes Huntress Salesforce Data (June 11–22, 2026)
🏷 Klue / Icarus Threat Actor  |  📅 June 11–22, 2026
Klue, a market intelligence SaaS platform used by many cybersecurity vendors, was compromised by Icarus — an extortion group that emerged in April 2026. The attack exploited a long-dormant OAuth credential originally created to test a third-party integration that was never deployed. On June 11, threat actors used this credential to push malicious code into Klue's backend, harvesting live OAuth tokens for all connected customer integrations. The stolen tokens were used to exfiltrate data from customers' Salesforce environments. Confirmed victims include Huntress, Recorded Future, Tanium, Jamf, Sprout Social, Gong, and others. Klue deactivated all OAuth integrations (Salesforce, HubSpot, SharePoint, Zoom, Slack, etc.) on June 12. Icarus listed Klue on its leak site June 21–22. Huntress independently disclosed the incident on its blog. IMPACT: SaaS-to-SaaS OAuth trust chains are an established, scalable exfiltration vector. This incident demonstrates that even mature, security-aware organizations (Huntress is itself an MDR vendor) are vulnerable when a trusted third-party SaaS integration is compromised. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Third-party SaaS integrations represent a growing blind spot; this is a direct SAT/MDR conversation opportunity — phishing simulation and MDR detection coverage for SaaS integration anomalies. Competitive note: Huntress (MDR competitor) suffered a third-party data breach; sales teams may encounter this in customer conversations.

🌐 DNS Protection (1 new item)

🆕 Infoblox Offers 15 Months for 12 to Cisco Umbrella Customers — Migration Incentive Ends July 31, 2026
🏷 Infoblox  |  📅 June 2026 (offer expires July 31, 2026)
Infoblox is offering 15 months of Infoblox Threat Defense for the price of 12 months to organizations migrating from Cisco Umbrella (now rebranded Cisco Secure Access – DNS Defense). Context: Cisco has been discontinuing the standalone Umbrella DNS product, folding customers into its broader SASE platform — Cisco Secure Access. The standalone DNS defense positioning is eroding. Cisco Secure Access – DNS Defense was benchmarked blocking only 39.3% of threats in recent independent testing, including gaps in DNS exfiltration, DGA, and lookalike domain detection. Infoblox is positioning its Threat Defense as a higher-efficacy, DNS-specialist alternative. The 15-for-12 promotion ends July 31, 2026, creating a time-bound displacement window. IMPACT: Cisco's DNS product consolidation is creating measurable churn opportunity in the DNS protection market. Infoblox is executing an aggressive pricing campaign to capture displaced Umbrella customers in this window. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: The DNS protection competitive landscape is actively shifting — Cisco is de-prioritizing standalone DNS, and Infoblox is capitalizing. Key evaluation window is open now through July 31. MSPs with Cisco Umbrella in their stack should be aware of the migration incentive their customers may encounter. Competitive implication for DNS positioning: Differentiation must emphasize efficacy metrics (threat detection rates, DGA coverage, DNS exfiltration detection) rather than brand recognition alone.

🔴 Competitive Threat Context: GentleKiller EDR Killer Targets 48 Security Vendors (1 strategic note)

🔴 GentleKiller Ransomware Framework Targets CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Microsoft, ESET, Kaspersky — 400+ Processes (June 18–21, 2026)
🏷 ESET Research  |  📅 June 18–21, 2026
ESET Research published an analysis of Gentlemen ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) and its GentleKiller EDR killer framework — a suite of 8 variants specifically designed to disable endpoint detection and response tools before ransomware deployment. GentleKiller abuses the BYOVD (Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver) technique to terminate 400+ security processes across 48 vendors, including CrowdStrike Falcon, SentinelOne Singularity, Microsoft Defender, ESET, and Kaspersky. Notably, Gentlemen has operationalized newly published PoC (proof-of-concept) BYOVD exploits from GitHub within days of public disclosure — demonstrating a highly agile, well-resourced development pipeline. The framework also integrates third-party tools (HexKiller, ThrottleBlood, HavocKiller). Unlike most RaaS operators who outsource EDR killing to affiliates, Gentlemen centralizes this capability to lower affiliate entry barriers. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: Ransomware actors are explicitly targeting the most prominent endpoint security vendors (our category) as technical obstacles to bypass. This validates the value of cloud-native, behavioral detection approaches that do not rely solely on process-level defense — and the need for MDR layers that can detect pre-encryption attacker behavior even if endpoint agents are killed. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Prospect questions about "what happens if your agent gets killed" are becoming more frequent. This is a direct sales differentiator for MDR with 24/7 behavioral monitoring that operates independently of the endpoint agent. See Threat Intel briefing 2026-06-22 for full technical details.
