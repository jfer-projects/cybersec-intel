🎓 Security Awareness Training / HRM (2 new items)

🆕 KnowBe4 Signs Multi-Year Strategic Collaboration Agreement with AWS (July 14, 2026)
🏷 KnowBe4 / AWS  |  📅 July 14, 2026
KnowBe4 announced a multi-year strategic collaboration agreement (SCA) with Amazon Web Services covering joint go-to-market initiatives, sales enablement, and customer adoption programs, and intended to simplify how organizations procure, deploy, and scale KnowBe4's human risk management platform through AWS. The agreement also aims to create new opportunities for KnowBe4's channel partner ecosystem. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: Hyperscaler SCAs increasingly function as procurement shortcuts (AWS Marketplace private offers, committed-spend drawdown) that can shorten SAT/HRM sales cycles for cloud-native buyers. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Worth confirming our own SAT offering's AWS/Azure/GCP marketplace posture, since a competitor's hyperscaler SCA can become a procurement-path advantage independent of product capability.
🆕 KnowBe4 Launches Custom AI Video Builder via Synthesia Partnership (July 22, 2026)
🏷 KnowBe4 / Synthesia  |  📅 July 22, 2026
Building on its existing Synthesia partnership, KnowBe4 launched Custom AI Video Builder, letting security admins generate studio-quality, narrated training videos and deploy them directly into their security awareness programs within minutes. The feature activates a prepaid Synthesia Starter license (roughly $250/year value, up to 120 minutes of narrated video annually) through KnowBe4's ModStore at no additional cost. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: Custom, AI-generated video content shifts SAT/HRM competition from "who has the biggest course library" toward "who lets admins produce relevant content fastest" — a capability that's harder to match without a comparable content-generation partnership already in place. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: A concrete, dated feature (with a bundled dollar value) to benchmark against if our own SAT roadmap includes AI-assisted content creation.

🏷 Jamf  |  📅 July 1, 2026 (newly identified, not previously reported)
Jamf made AI Governance generally available within Jamf for Mac, adding native macOS controls for IT and security teams to discover AI-tool usage, apply policy, and generate audit-ready reports across managed Mac fleets — built into the OS-level agent rather than shipped as a separate proxy or bolt-on agent. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: Worth a quick internal check on whether AI-tool discovery/governance is represented on our own endpoint roadmap.

📌 Strategic Context: "AI" Competitive Pressure Is No Longer Confined to the SOC

The last several weeks of competitive moves centered on AI inside the security operations workflow — autonomous resolution rates, agentic investigation, agentic SOC platforms. This week's items show the same AI-investment pattern spreading outward: KnowBe4 is applying it to training-content generation (Custom AI Video Builder), Jamf is applying it to governance of end-user AI tool usage rather than to detection itself, and Sophos is converting its SOC-automation story into third-party analyst validation. None of these are detection-engine claims — they're adjacent capabilities (content creation, governance, credibility) built around the same underlying AI investment. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: As "AI-powered" claims proliferate across every category, differentiation is likely to shift toward which vendor can point to independent validation (analyst placements, benchmarks) or concrete, bundled feature value (like KnowBe4's dollar-denominated Synthesia license) rather than the word "AI" on its own — worth keeping that lens on our own AI-related messaging across all six products.
