🛡️ Endpoint Protection (2 new items)

🆕 Sophos Launches Sophos Fusion — AI-Native Cybersecurity Defense System (July 15, 2026)
🏷 Sophos  |  📅 July 15, 2026
Sophos announced Sophos Fusion, described as the industry's first and most complete "AI-native cybersecurity defense system," designed to use agentic AI to connect and synchronize every control point — endpoint, network, email, and cloud — across a customer's environment. Sophos says it now operates the world's largest agentic SOC, covering more than 40,000 customers, with 52% of security cases resolved entirely by AI and an average alert-to-fully-automated-response time of 89 seconds. Fusion is expected to become generally available beginning October 2026. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: This is a platform-level repositioning, not a single-product update — Sophos is explicitly selling a unified "defense system" narrative that spans endpoint protection and MDR simultaneously, following its Secureworks acquisition. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: The 52% autonomous-resolution and 89-second response-time figures are concrete, citable benchmarks; worth checking whether our own MDR SOC has comparable metrics ready for competitive conversations, since Sophos is clearly making autonomous resolution rate its headline differentiator.
🆕 Kaspersky Adds AI-Driven Phishing and List-Linking Attack Detection to Email Security (July 13, 2026)
🏷 Kaspersky  |  📅 July 13, 2026
Kaspersky released an update to its email security product adding advanced heuristics aimed at AI-generated phishing content and new detection for "list-linking" attacks — a technique where an attacker subscribes a victim's email address to large numbers of mailing lists to bury legitimate mail and set up a pretext for a follow-on help-desk or vishing social-engineering attempt. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: A modest, incremental release, but confirms AI-generated phishing detection is becoming table-stakes across email/endpoint security vendors rather than a distinguishing feature. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: List-linking/subscription-bombing as a vishing setup technique is a useful, concrete scenario to check against our own SAT phishing-simulation and email-filtering content for coverage gaps.

🌐 DNS Protection (1 new item)

🆕 Infoblox Runs Time-Boxed Switch-from-Cisco Promotion, Publishes 39.3% Threat-Block Rate Claim Against Cisco Secure Access (Offer Ends July 31, 2026)
🏷 Infoblox / Cisco  |  📅 Active through July 31, 2026
Infoblox is offering customers switching from Cisco Secure Access (formerly Cisco Umbrella) 15 months of Infoblox Threat Defense for the price of 12, with the offer expiring July 31, 2026. Infoblox is backing the promotion with published head-to-head testing via its own "Infoblox Inspect" tool, claiming Cisco Secure Access — DNS Defense blocked only 39.3% of threats in its test sample, citing specific gaps in DNS exfiltration detection, domain generation algorithm (DGA) coverage, and lookalike-domain identification. COMPETITIVE IMPLICATION: This follows directly on Infoblox's July 8 announcement to acquire Kentik and its May Axur acquisition — Infoblox is pairing platform expansion with an aggressive, dated pricing incentive aimed squarely at the ongoing Cisco Umbrella-to-Secure-Access migration wave. PORTFOLIO ANGLE: A vendor-published, named-competitor detection-rate claim (39.3%) is likely to surface in DNS protection sales conversations regardless of which vendors are actually being compared; worth having an independently sourced or internally validated DNS threat-detection benchmark on hand rather than relying on any single vendor's self-reported number.

📌 Strategic Context: Autonomous Resolution Rate Is Becoming the New Headline Metric

A lighter week for new competitive material, but the two substantive items both point toward the same shift: vendors are increasingly leading with an autonomous-resolution or detection-rate number rather than a feature list. Sophos is selling Fusion on a 52% autonomous-case-resolution figure and an 89-second alert-to-response time; Infoblox is selling its DNS switch promotion on a specific (and unflattering to Cisco) 39.3% detection-rate claim. Both are attempts to make an otherwise abstract "our AI/detection is better" pitch concrete and comparable. PORTFOLIO IMPLICATION: As this pattern spreads, prospects and MSP partners will increasingly ask for a specific, citable number — autonomous resolution rate, mean time to detect/respond, or detection-rate percentage — rather than accepting qualitative claims. Worth confirming each of our six products has at least one externally defensible metric of this kind ready for competitive conversations before this becomes the default expectation industry-wide.
